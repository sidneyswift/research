# "Meta-Meta-Prompting: The Secret to Making AI Agents Work" (`garrytan`, essay)

> Essay #6 of Garry Tan's 8-part "AI Explainer" series. The recursion essay: the system that runs Garry's life "didn't exist as a monolith — it was assembled from skills, and those skills were themselves created by a skill." Our primary source for **skillify-as-meta-skill** ("skills that build skills"), the **book-mirror** personalization pipeline and its iterative fact-check hardening, **entity propagation** across a ~100,000-page brain, and the answer to "how do you prompt your AI?" — *"I don't. The skills are the prompts."* The clearest first-person statement of the [[patterns/behavioral/skill-as-method-call]] and [[patterns/behavioral/diarization]] ideas, all grounded in real `gbrain` skills we verified in the snapshot.

## Why we cite this

- **The richest narration of [[patterns/behavioral/skill-as-method-call]]** in the series. book-mirror is a *parameterized markdown procedure* — same skill, different book in, different personalized page out — and the essay's *"the skills are the prompts"* ([[#skills-are-the-prompts]]) is the thesis sentence for that pattern. Grounded by the real `book-mirror/SKILL.md` (parameterized: any EPUB/PDF → `media/books/<slug>-personalized.md`).
- **The canonical first-person statement of [[patterns/behavioral/diarization]].** "Entity propagation" ([[#brain-100k]]) is diarization by another name: after every meeting the model *reads everything about each entity and writes one distilled, structured page* (compiled truth + append-only timeline). Grounded by `meeting-ingestion/SKILL.md` ("EVERY attendee gets a people page… entity propagation… timeline merge").
- **A working demonstration of [[patterns/quality-bar/complexity-ratchet]].** The book-mirror v1→v3 story ([[#book-mirror-iteration]]) is the ratchet in one workflow: a caught error becomes a *baked-in* fact-check step, *"and every mirror since has been clean."* Forward-only quality.
- **Skillify framed as a *meta-skill* (skills that build skills)** ([[#skills-that-build-skills]]) — the "meta-meta-prompting" of the title. This is the recursion layer above [[patterns/quality-bar/skill-pack-bundle]]: the skill-pack is the artifact; skillify is the factory that mints skill-packs and wires them into the resolver. Grounded by `skillify/SKILL.md` ("The meta skill", 11-item checklist incl. resolver trigger + resolver eval + check-resolvable).
- **The clearest plain-language statement of [[patterns/structural/thin-harness-fat-skills]].** The "The Architecture" section ([[#architecture]]) literally enumerates *thin harness / fat skills / fat data / fat code / interchangeable models*, naming OpenClaw as "a few thousand lines of routing logic… It just routes." This is the series' core architecture stated without metaphor.
- **[[patterns/composition/resolver-routing-table]], the personal-AI variant.** Skillify "registers it in the resolver" and "run check_resolvable to verify the new skill is wired into the resolver" ([[#how-to-start]]) — both grounded by real gbrain artifacts (`skills/RESOLVER.md`, the shipped `check-resolvable` CLI command).
- **[[patterns/behavioral/latent-vs-deterministic-split]].** book-mirror's *"the CLI is the trusted runtime; the skill is the orchestration prose"* (a deterministic CLI fanning out read-only latent subagents) is one of the cleanest split examples in either repo — and the essay's `#models-interchangeable` line ("the skill decides which model") is the routing half of the same idea.
- **A mid-series GStack popularity datapoint (87,000+ stars)** that pins this essay on the star-growth time-series between the series-early and essay-#8 figures.
- **A documented attribution and convergence note**: OpenClaw is named as a harness alongside Hermes Agent (consistent with the essay-#8 correction that OpenClaw is Steinberger's, [[sources/garrytan--foxconn-factories#openclaw]]); and GBrain is credited to *Karpathy's LLM Wiki*, which is this wiki's own foundational pattern.

## Contradictions & tensions (for LINT)

1. **Internal cron-count inconsistency.** Same essay: *"more than 100 crons per day"* and *"100 cronjobs 24/7"* ([[#crons]]) vs *"15 cron jobs"* in the summary line ([[#why-2am]]). Likely "15 cron *definitions* that fan out to 100+ scheduled runs/day," but the essay never reconciles it. Author self-claim either way — cite the specific anchor, not a blended number.
2. **Internal skill-count inconsistency.** *"Over 100 of them"* ([[#architecture]]) vs *"39 installable skills"* ([[#how-to-start]]) vs *"30+ installable skillpacks"* ([[#fat-skills-thin-harness-coda]]). Reconciliation: 100+ is Garry's *private* running set; 30–39 is the *open-source installable* subset (the gbrain snapshot ships 53 `skills/` entries, [[sources/garrytan--gbrain#root]]). Not a true contradiction once the private/public distinction is drawn, but the bare "100+ skills" headline overstates what a reader can actually install.
3. **Personal-brain page count vs shipped-repo page count.** Essay's *"100,000 pages"* ([[#brain-100k]]) is Garry's personal brain; the gbrain repo banner says *17,888 pages* (2026-05-20, [[sources/garrytan--gbrain#root]]). Two different objects — flag so a future reader doesn't cite "gbrain has 100K pages."
4. **OpenClaw authorship (consistent with the standing correction).** This essay names OpenClaw and Hermes Agent as the harnesses ([[#open-source-stack]]) without attributing OpenClaw to anyone; essay #8 states *"Peter Steinberger built OpenClaw"* ([[sources/garrytan--foxconn-factories#openclaw]]). No new contradiction, but reinforces the open correction that `garrytan/openclaw` is most likely a fork/deployment, not Garry's original. New named harness **Hermes Agent** and new bootstrap tool **Pi** ([[#how-to-start]]) appear here for the first time in the series — track as entities.
5. **Tokenmaxxing vs. cost-rationing (cross-essay tension).** This essay's whole posture — 100+ daily crons, ~100K-page brain, fanning a subagent per book chapter, three frontier models per cross-modal eval — is *tokenmaxxing* in practice, aligned with essay #8's explicit spend-freely thesis ([[sources/garrytan--foxconn-factories#tokenmaxxing]]) and in tension with gbrain's own `[AGENT]`-banner that forces an operator to confront a ~25× cost spread before proceeding ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#AGENTS.md]]). Same standing open question; this essay is evidence on the *spend* side.
6. **"The skills are the prompts" vs. the snapshot reality of CLI-backed skills.** The essay frames skills as pure markdown prompts ([[#skills-are-the-prompts]]), but the real `book-mirror` skill is explicit that *"book-mirror runs as a CLI command… NOT as a pure markdown skill"* — the markdown is "orchestration prose around" a trusted deterministic runtime. Not a contradiction so much as a *useful correction to the essay's own simplification*: the great skills are markdown-over-deterministic-code, not markdown alone. Worth surfacing wherever we quote "the skills are the prompts."
