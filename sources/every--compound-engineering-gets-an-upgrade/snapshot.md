## Thesis: the loop grew from 4 steps to 8

The essay narrates how compound engineering **evolved from a four-step cycle to an
eight-step process** as models got more capable — the "work" phase shrank in
importance and human judgment migrated to the two *ends* of the loop.

**Original four-step loop (verbatim fragment):**

> "Brainstorm → work → review → compound → repeat."

**Expanded eight-step loop (verbatim fragment):**

> "Ideate → brainstorm → plan → work → review → polish → compound → repeat"

---

## Opening (verbatim fragment)

> "In its early days, compound engineering was mostly about the code. I wanted to see
> if I could get an AI model to make a plan, do the work the way I wanted it done,
> review the results against my standards, and incorporate lessons from my feedback
> so it wouldn't make the same mistake next time."

---

## Why "work" became less central (verbatim fragment)

> "the work phase has become boring—in the best way. If the plan is good and the
> agent has the right context, it usually does the work right."

---

## The sandwich analogy (verbatim fragment)

> "AI is the stuff in the middle. Humans are the bread on either end, holding it
> together."

The two new phases **bookend** the original loop — human judgment at the front
(deciding what's worth building) and at the back (deciding whether it feels right).

---

## The two new phases (verbatim fragments)

**Front end — Ideate / Brainstorm (the "what to build" decision):**

> "I need to decide what is worth building. I need to understand the user, the
> product, the weird edge cases, and the thing that feels exciting enough to spend
> time on."

**End — Polish (the "does it feel right" judgment):**

> "I click around and look at the design. I read the copy. I ask whether the
> experience feels right."

---

## Generalization beyond engineering (verbatim fragment)

> "The guide is about engineering, but I think the pattern applies to knowledge work
> much more broadly."

(I.e. automation handles the middle; human expertise stays load-bearing at the
beginning — strategy/decision — and the end — quality/intuition.)
