# Original notes on documentation-use logs

Read: arXiv:2310.10817v2 (2024-02-29), Daye Nam, Andrew Macvean, Brad Myers, Bogdan Vasilescu, CHI 2024. Retrieved 2026-11-05. Reviewed abstract, methods/data/ethics/limitations (sections 3.1-3.4), results and discussion (5-6), conclusion. Full PDF downloaded locally; this is original paraphrase, not a full-paper reproduction.

Four Google cloud services, over 100,000 users in the reported study; observations centered on May 2020 and adjacent history/follow-up windows. The authors cluster documentation-use patterns and model associations with experience, product type, previous documentation use and later API calls. Product/platform experience differs; guides are not just for novices. A reported association between guide visits and later API use is not a causal conversion uplift.

Discussion 6.2 recommends explicit audiences, important information across entry paths (or prominent functional links), and product-specific starting recommendations within consistent shared site navigation. This creates a useful tension with blanket anti-duplication advice. Some product labels in the discussion appear inconsistent with other sections; do not infer which anonymized product is which. Discussion 6.3 proposes personalization as future vision, not an evaluated implementation.

Limitations: signed-in users, pseudonymized aggregated logs, monthly granularity, a limited time window, one vendor and four services. Logs cannot establish intent, comprehension, success of a specific integration or causation; may omit browsing while signed out and off-site sources. Short sessions are filtered; dwell time is not a direct task-success measure. Use logs to target interviews and task tests, not to replace them.
