# diarization

> The model reads *everything* about one subject, holds the contradictions in mind, notices what changed and when, and writes **one structured page of distilled judgment** — an analyst's brief, not a database lookup. Its defining output is something no SQL query, keyword filter, or embedding search can produce: the gap between what someone *says* and what they're *actually building*.

## Longer definition

Diarization is the latent step that turns a pile of documents (transcripts, emails, commits, tweets, prior notes) into a single compiled page about a person, company, meeting, book, or topic. Garry Tan names it as the fifth of his five primitives and frames it by its limit case: *"The model reads everything about a subject and writes a structured profile — a single page of judgment distilled from dozens or hundreds of documents… the difference between a database lookup and an analyst's brief. No SQL query produces this. No RAG pipeline produces this."* ([[sources/garrytan--thin-harness-fat-skills#diarization]]). What makes it *judgment* rather than *retrieval* is that the page's value lives in the things only synthesis surfaces: a contradiction held across sources (`SAYS: "Datadog for AI agents" / ACTUALLY BUILDING: 80% of commits in the billing module → a FinOps tool disguised as observability`), or a reclassification that no embedding captures (a founder who "applied as developer tools" but whose transcript reveals SOC2 compliance work → move to FinTech/RegTech) ([[sources/garrytan--thin-harness-fat-skills#enrich-founder]], [[sources/garrytan--thin-harness-fat-skills#match-skills]]). The pattern operates on *many sources about one subject* and produces *a compounding compiled page* (each diarized page enriches the next). A creator reaches for it whenever the load-bearing signal is a synthesis — a "says vs. does" gap, a trajectory, a held belief — that retrieval is structurally blind to.

The hard, error-prone part is **attribution**: *who* holds a belief — the author, someone they quote, or the system's own inference. This is where diarization is most easily done wrong, and the wiki tracks it as the pattern's signature failure mode (see Mechanism and Open questions).

## Mechanism

The essays articulate diarization; **gbrain is where you can see it shipped as real files** ([[artifacts/plugins/gbrain]]). Four concrete pieces:

1. **The skill states the bar explicitly.** `skills/enrich/SKILL.md` demands an intelligence dossier, not a scrape: *"A brain page should read like an intelligence dossier, not a LinkedIn scrape. Facts are table stakes. Texture is the value — what do they believe, what are they building, what makes them tick, where are they headed"* ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#skill-enrich]]). It maps signal *types* to *sections* — "Opinions, beliefs → What They Believe", "Current projects → What They're Building", "Ascending/plateauing/pivoting? → Trajectory" — which is the "says vs. does" surface rendered as a page schema. Crucially, "When sources conflict, note the contradiction with both citations" — the contradiction is kept, not flattened.

2. **The page schema is compiled-truth-on-top + append-only-timeline-below + raw sidecars.** The brain-page shape is *"compiled truth at the top, an append-only timeline below, raw-data sidecars"* ([[sources/garrytan--meta-meta-prompting#brain-100k]]); `enrich`'s contract guarantees *"every enriched page has compiled truth (State section) with inline citations"* plus a dated timeline, and stores raw API responses via `put_raw_data` so *"if the compiled truth is ever questioned, the raw data shows exactly what the API returned"* ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#skill-enrich]]). This is the analyst's-brief structure: a synthesized judgment layer sitting over an immutable evidence layer.

3. **Entity propagation makes diarization compound.** `skills/meeting-ingestion/SKILL.md` runs a mandatory propagation pass: *"EVERY attendee gets a people page (created or updated)… EVERY company discussed gets entity propagation… Timeline entries on ALL mentioned entities (timeline merge)"*, with a "Phase 4: Entity propagation (MANDATORY)" step ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#skill-meeting-ingestion]]). The essay restates it: *"after every meeting, the system walks through every person and company mentioned and updates their brain pages"* ([[sources/garrytan--meta-meta-prompting#brain-100k]]). One diarization event re-diarizes the graph it touched — the "filing cabinet vs. nervous system" distinction.

4. **The holder-confusion fix encodes the attribution discipline.** `docs/takes-vs-facts.md` is an epistemological layer that records *"WHO believes WHAT, with confidence weight and time"* — "Multi-holder — captures beliefs from *any* speaker, not just the brain owner", with kinds `take` / `fact` / `bet` / `hunch` and explicit holders (`holder=people/jared-friedman`, `holder=world`, `holder=brain`) ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#docs-takes-vs-facts]]). The companion `docs/contradictions.md` plus a cross-model LLM judge (`src/core/eval-contradictions/judge.ts`) measure how often unmarked contradictions surface in retrieval. This is the machinery behind the ratchet story: gbrain's epistemological extraction was *"holder confusion wrong 35% of the time in v1"*, fixed in v2 across six failure modes with **17 locked tests** ([[sources/garrytan--complexity-ratchet#gbrain-extraction-ratchet]]) — because *"if you're building a system that tracks what people believe, you need to know WHO believes it"* ([[sources/garrytan--complexity-ratchet#holder-confusion]]).

**Note the layering.** Diarization sits *on top of* a deterministic substrate, but is not itself deterministic. The typed-link graph beneath it is mechanical — back-links auto-materialize on every `put_page` with "zero LLM calls" ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#README]]) — yet the one-page *synthesis* is irreducibly latent. The graph is the filing cabinet; diarization is the judgment written into it. This is exactly the [[patterns/behavioral/latent-vs-deterministic-split]] applied to a knowledge base: deterministic edges, latent compilation.

## When to use

- **Knowledge work whose value is synthesis-with-judgment over many sources about one subject** — people, companies, meetings, books, topics. If the deliverable is "an analyst's brief on X," diarize.
- **When the key signal is a *contradiction* or a *gap* that retrieval cannot see** — "says vs. does," a hallucinated-positioning vs. actual-commits mismatch, a self-description that the evidence contradicts ([[sources/garrytan--thin-harness-fat-skills#enrich-founder]]).
- **When you're building a *compounding* knowledge base** — each diarized page enriches the next, and entity propagation re-diarizes the neighborhood on every new event ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#skill-meeting-ingestion]]).
- **When you need a stable, citable judgment layer over volatile evidence** — the compiled-truth-over-timeline-over-raw shape gives you a synthesis you can quote *and* the provenance to defend it ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#skill-enrich]]).

## When NOT to use

- **When a precise lookup or an aggregate answers the question.** "What's Acme's MRR?" or "list every meeting with X in March" is a SQL/search job. Forcing it through a latent synthesis is the [[patterns/behavioral/latent-vs-deterministic-split]] error in its most expensive form — slower, costlier, and *less* reliable than the deterministic path. Diarization is for judgment, not facts you can index.
- **When the sources are too thin to support judgment.** `enrich` guards this directly: *"No stubs: every new page has meaningful content"*, and its "Do NOT enrich" list includes "Random mentions with no relationship signal" and bot/spam accounts ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#skill-enrich]]). Diarizing a subject you have almost nothing on doesn't produce a thin brief — it produces a *fabricated* one. Notability-gate first.
- **When attribution must be exact but you can't yet pin *who* holds each claim.** If your pipeline can't distinguish "the author believes X" from "the author quotes someone who believes X" from "the system inferred X," you will ship the holder-confusion bug — wrong 35% of the time in gbrain v1 ([[sources/garrytan--complexity-ratchet#gbrain-extraction-ratchet]]). Ship the holder guard (the takes-vs-facts split + a contradiction/holder eval) before you ship diarized advice that downstream agents act on.
- **When the synthesis won't be re-read or compounded.** A one-off "summarize these docs" with no compiled page, no timeline, and no propagation is just summarization. Diarization earns its structure only when the page persists and feeds the next pass.

## Why it works

LLMs are uniquely good at exactly the step diarization isolates: reading heterogeneous text and producing a judgment that *integrates* it — holding two conflicting sources in mind and naming the tension rather than averaging it away. Retrieval systems, by construction, cannot do this. RAG, vector similarity, keyword search, and a plain DB lookup all *return passages or rows*; none of them *decide* that "80% of commits in billing" contradicts "Datadog for AI agents" and re-label the company. The essay states it as a hard limit — *"No SQL query produces this. No RAG pipeline produces this"* ([[sources/garrytan--thin-harness-fat-skills#diarization]]) — and the matching essay drives it home with the reclassification case: *"No embedding captures the Kim reclassification"* ([[sources/garrytan--thin-harness-fat-skills#match-skills]]). The naked-models essay supplies the epistemic frame: *"A naked model produces plausible text. A harnessed system produces verified text"* ([[sources/garrytan--naked-models#verified-vs-plausible]]) — and diarization is the harnessed version, because the compiled page is checked against cited raw sidecars and a contradiction judge, not asserted from nothing. The compounding property is why it's worth the latent cost: a diarized page is reused by every later workflow (meeting prep pulled an accumulated brain page, AGI-timeline beliefs, and conversation hooks in under two minutes — *"The system prepped not just facts, but angles"* — [[sources/garrytan--meta-meta-prompting#brain-100k]]), so the synthesis is amortized across many reads while the deterministic substrate keeps it cheap to maintain.

> **Why still `proposed`, not `confirmed`:** diarization is grounded in exactly **one in-wiki artifact** (gbrain), from **one creator** (Garry Tan). The mechanism is richly evidenced there — five separate source anchors and four real files — but a single artifact, however well-instrumented, is a house style, not yet a cross-creator pattern. **Promotion needs a 2nd, independent example: a different pack that ships diarization-style synthesis** — read-everything-about-one-subject → one compiled page of judgment (compiled truth + timeline), ideally with its own *holder/attribution* guard — authored by someone other than Garry. The gstack-vs-gbrain "two distinct artifacts" trick does **not** rescue this one: gstack is a *coding* skill framework and does not ship a diarization capability, so it cannot serve as the second example. The distinctive thing a second example must demonstrate is the *judgment-over-retrieval* output (a held contradiction, a reclassification), not merely "a summary page."

## Counter-examples or anti-pattern

- **RAG / vector similarity / keyword search / a plain DB lookup — the essays' explicit foils.** These are the things diarization is defined *against*: *"No SQL query produces this. No RAG pipeline produces this"* ([[sources/garrytan--thin-harness-fat-skills#diarization]]); *"No embedding captures the Kim reclassification"* ([[sources/garrytan--thin-harness-fat-skills#match-skills]]). They retrieve passages and rows; they do not hold contradictions in mind and synthesize a judgment. Reaching for one of them where the answer requires *held judgment* (a says-vs-does gap, a trajectory call) is the failure — you get plausible passages, not a verified brief.
- **The inverse failure (latent-where-deterministic-suffices).** Using diarization where a precise lookup or aggregate would do is the opposite error and just as real: it's the [[patterns/behavioral/latent-vs-deterministic-split]] mistake. gbrain itself draws the line — it does *not* diarize "what's Acme's MRR"; that's a typed `fact` with a holder and a number, and the deterministic graph + `facts` table answer it. Diarization is reserved for the synthesis layer (`takes`, compiled truth), and conflating the two is the category error `takes-vs-facts.md` exists to prevent ([[sources/garrytan--gbrain#docs-takes-vs-facts]]).
- **Holder confusion (the within-pattern anti-pattern).** Diarizing *without* attribution — collapsing "X believes" / "X quotes Y believing" / "the system inferred" into one undifferentiated claim — is diarization done wrong, and it was wrong **35% of the time** in gbrain v1 before the holder guard + 17 locked tests ([[sources/garrytan--complexity-ratchet#gbrain-extraction-ratchet]]). A diarized page that asserts beliefs without saying *whose* they are is a confident, well-formatted brief that is one-third fiction.

## Open questions

- **Single-artifact, single-creator grounding.** Everything here is gbrain, and gbrain is Garry Tan's. Does diarization appear as a *named, shipped* capability anywhere else in the ecosystem — an Anthropic skill, a third-party pack — or is "diarization" a Garry-coined frame for a thing others build without naming? Until a non-Garry example lands, treat the pattern as well-evidenced-but-local.
- **How much of the quality is schema vs. synthesis?** The compiled-truth + timeline + raw-sidecar *structure* is largely deterministic; the *judgment written into it* is latent. We don't yet know the split — would a weaker model produce a nearly-as-good page given the same rigid schema (the schema is doing the work), or does the page quality track the model's synthesis ability (the prompt is doing the work)? This bears directly on the [[patterns/behavioral/latent-vs-deterministic-split]] boundary and on how reusable the pattern is across model tiers.
- **Is the holder guard transferable, or gbrain-specific?** gbrain's fix is a typed `takes` table with explicit holders + a cross-model contradiction judge. Is that the *general* answer to attribution in diarization, or one implementation among several? A second example with a *different* holder-confusion mechanism would tell us whether "ship the attribution guard first" is a universal sub-rule or a gbrain idiom.
- **Where's the line between diarization and ordinary summarization?** The working distinction is "produces judgment retrieval can't (held contradiction, reclassification) + persists + compounds." But that's a soft boundary. A sharper test — ideally one a resolver or an eval could apply — would help decide whether a given "summary page" skill is really an instance of this pattern.
